# 11. CONCLUSION

The aim of this fellowship project was to build a system that can screen how groundwater contamination from a hypothetical ISR uranium operation would behave in Jharkhand, check what the existing groundwater record says, find where monitoring is weakest, and inform the people concerned. The project achieved this through JalDrishti, a web-based decision-support system that is deployed and working.

The most important step of the project was a change of method. The first five months followed the proposal and trained machine learning models on measured and synthetic water-quality data. In June 2026 a careful check showed that these models were not learning how contamination spreads, because the measured data had no information about distance or time. The prediction core was rebuilt as a physics-based contaminant transport engine, using the Domenico and Ogata–Banks solutions adapted for fractured hard rock, with dual-porosity slowing, matrix diffusion, a leach zone, restoration based on Texas ISR records, uranium attenuation and the NUREG-1569 2-of-3 excursion test. The engine uses real Jharkhand data: aquifer maps, a groundwater flow field from 398 CGWB stations (confirmed by a 32-year record), a fracture field from GSI lineaments, NAQUIM layers, uranium deposit grades and ore depths, and, in the uranium belt, the permeability measured in the belt’s own rock, checked against the water pumped out of the mines, with local uranium and radium backgrounds from BARC surveys. Every run gives two answers: one for the rock as measured, and one, labelled hypothetical, for rock permeable enough for ISR. An XGBoost surrogate trained on 18,000 engine scenarios gives fast answers with calibrated 80% uncertainty bands.

The main results are:

- The ML surrogate (version 6) reproduces the physics engine with an R² (log scale) of 0.89 to 0.97, performs better than all baseline models, and keeps its 80% coverage on real-location test cases. The radium travel-distance result does not meet the target and is reported openly.
- The engine matches an exact numerical solution after a missing formula term was found and restored, and its permeability in the belt lies on the cautious side of the mines’ own discharge records.
- In the measured record, uranium is within its limit at every tested well, while nitrate (22 wells) and fluoride (32 wells) are the real health concerns, in 14 of 24 districts. Iron, arsenic and manganese have never been tested.
- At the hypothetical Jaduguda site, in the rock as measured, uranium spreads about 4 m beyond the wellfield in 20 years, sulphate 11 m and TDS 30 m, and no excursion is declared. If the rock were permeable enough for ISR, TDS would spread about 535 m. At a more permeable belt site near Narwapahar, chloride and TDS declare an excursion in year 19 while uranium is still at background at the ring, so the chemical indicators, not uranium, give the first warning.
- The shallow-aquifer screening at Jaduguda gives a low index (0.05); the first constituent to reach the shallow aquifer is TDS, after about 140 years, while uranium would take thousands of years.
- The 25 most poorly monitored blocks all lie in the uranium belt.
- The alert system raises 53 tiered alerts from the measured data and delivers them to residents by email.
- The system is supported by 463 engine tests and 533 backend tests and runs online with five secure user roles.

The project also has clear limits. No real ISR plume exists in Jharkhand, so the model cannot be validated against a measured plume, and its uncertainty bands cover only the uncertainty of the input values, not errors in the model itself. With the permeability now taken from the belt’s own rock, the inputs that control the plume extent most are the fracture–matrix exchange terms, the transfer rate ω and the storage ratio β, and neither has been measured anywhere in the shear zone. No pumping test has been made at a deposit itself. Trend forecasting of health-related water quality was not possible, because no repeated measurements exist. The system works on the manual monitoring network; it is a decision-support tool ready for sensors, not a live sensor network.

In conclusion, JalDrishti shows that a useful and honest groundwater contamination screening tool can be built for hard-rock India from open data, standard groundwater physics and machine learning, and deployed at low cost. Its main strength is not only the model but the discipline around it: every modelled number comes with its uncertainty and its source, measured and modelled results are kept apart, missing data is never shown as safe, and the places where more monitoring is needed are clearly identified.

# 12. FUTURE SCOPE

JalDrishti provides a base for more advanced groundwater monitoring and screening in the mining sector. The current prototype works well within the limits of the available data. Several improvements can make it more accurate and more useful.

## 12.1 Field Measurements in the Uranium Belt

The sensitivity analysis showed that, in the tight rock measured in the belt, the plume extent depends mostly on the fracture–matrix exchange terms (the transfer rate ω and the storage ratio β), which have never been measured in the Singhbhum shear zone. The permeability itself comes from a test 3 km from Turamdih, not from a deposit.

Possible work includes:

- A tracer test in the belt to measure ω and β; this is the single most valuable missing measurement.
- A pumping test at a deposit itself, to replace the nearest measured value.
- A packer test in the shear zone to measure fracture opening and matrix diffusion.
- Rock porosity for the Singhbhum schists: a published study measured it on 16 rocks from the shear zone, but its full text could not be obtained, so the model still uses literature values.
- Deep piezometers under the belt to measure the upward head difference that controls the shallow-aquifer screening.
- Updating the model inputs and retraining the surrogate with the new values.

## 12.2 Better Monitoring Data

Many of the gaps found in this project can be closed with low-cost changes to the sampling programme.

Recommended steps include:

- Testing the 55 existing Singhbhum samples for uranium.
- Adding iron, manganese and arsenic to the statewide testing panel.
- Repeating sampling at the same wells every year, so that trends and rate-of-change alerts become possible.
- Recording well depth and screen depth with every sample.
- Loading the 1994–2026 water-level record into the portal, and checking the 2024 nitrate values against CGWB’s own report before they are used for alerts.
- Adding wells in the 53 blocks with no well and level stations in the 83 blocks with none, starting from the priority ranking.

## 12.3 Integration of Real-Time Sensors

The data layer of JalDrishti is ready to accept new data. If water-quality or water-level sensors are installed in the belt, the system can become a true cyber-physical system [@lee2008].

Possible applications include:

- Automatic intake of sensor readings through a data interface
- Live alerts when a sensor value crosses a limit
- Comparison of sensor trends with the modelled plume

## 12.4 Water Quality Trend Forecasting

When repeated measurements of nitrate, fluoride and other health substances become available, the existing trend module can be extended to forecast water quality and to raise alerts when values rise quickly.

## 12.5 Improved Radium Model

A two-stage ML model can be built for radium: the first stage decides whether radium moves at all, and the second estimates how far. This is expected to fix the low accuracy of the current radium surrogate.

## 12.6 Cross-Check with a Numerical Groundwater Model

A detailed numerical model (such as MODFLOW with MT3D, or a discrete fracture network model) can be built for one site to measure how far the analytical engine differs from a more complete model.

## 12.7 More Ways to Reach Residents

Many residents in rural Jharkhand may not use email. Future versions can add:

- SMS alerts
- Alerts in Hindi and other regional languages
- A mobile-friendly app for field officers and residents
- Paid hosting, so that the scheduler never sleeps

## 12.8 Other Mining Contexts

The chemical list in the engine is modular. The system can be extended to other mining problems, such as acid drainage from coal mines (sulphate, iron and manganese), after the model is checked for those conditions.

### Future Vision

The long-term vision of this project is a groundwater safety platform for the mining regions of Jharkhand that combines field measurements, sensors, physics-based models and machine learning. Such a platform can help regulators plan monitoring before any new mining begins, give residents timely and clear information about the water they drink, and support safe and sustainable mining in the state.
