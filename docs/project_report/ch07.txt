# 7. RESULTS & ACHIEVEMENTS

JalDrishti was successfully developed, tested and deployed during the fellowship. The project met its three main objectives: a physics-based prediction model with a calibrated ML surrogate (O1), a data-gap analysis with monitoring recommendations (O2), and a working decision-support prototype (O3). The results fall into three kinds, and they are kept separate throughout this chapter:

- **Measured results:** laboratory values and water-level readings compared with published limits. Nothing is modelled.
- **Modelled results:** outputs of the engine or the surrogate for a hypothetical ISR operation. These are “what if” estimates, never forecasts of real contamination.
- **Validation results:** checks of the surrogate against the engine, or of the engine against an exact solution.

## 7.1 Measured Groundwater Quality (IS 10500:2012)

The 2023 CGWB record of 397 wells [@cgwb_gwq] was checked against IS 10500:2012 [@is10500]. The main findings for the health-related substances are shown below.

TABLE: Health-related results in the 2023 CGWB record (397 wells)
|{w=2.6,2.2,3.0,2.8,5.4}|
|Substance|Wells tested|Maximum|Limit|Wells above limit|
|Uranium|342|28.5 ppb|30 ppb|**0**|
|Nitrate|393|121 mg/L|45 mg/L (no relaxation)|**22**|
|Fluoride|397|1.91 mg/L|1.0 / 1.5 mg/L|**32** above acceptable, **11** above permissible|

Uranium is within its 30 ppb limit at every one of the 342 wells where it was tested (maximum 28.5 ppb at Sukurhutu, Ranchi). The health problems in Jharkhand’s groundwater today are nitrate and fluoride. Nitrate reaches 121 mg/L at Biru (Simdega), 2.7 times the limit, and 99 mg/L at Lowadih (Ranchi). Fluoride reaches 1.91 mg/L at Baresad (Latehar). In total, **32 wells** have a health exceedance, and **14 of 24 districts** have at least one such well. Hardness, calcium and magnesium are above their acceptable limits at a third to two-thirds of wells, but this is natural hard-rock chemistry and is reported separately.

The record also shows important gaps. Iron and arsenic were tested at none of the 397 wells, and manganese has no column. In the three Singhbhum districts (the uranium belt), 55 wells were sampled but never tested for uranium: 28 in East Singhbhum, 11 in Saraikela-Kharsawan and 16 in West Singhbhum. A published study near two Singhbhum deposits found iron up to 5.3 mg/L and manganese up to 1.3 mg/L, above the drinking-water limits [@giri2012], so these untested metals are a real concern in the belt.

A hydrochemical check showed that all 393 complete analyses in the 2023 file balance within ±3.2%. This is better than any normal laboratory batch, and it is explained by sodium having been calculated from the other ions. So the file’s charge balance cannot be used as proof of laboratory quality. In the older 2000–2021 record, the check is meaningful: 127 of 753 analyses were flagged as suspect.

### Key achievements include:

- The full measured record was assessed against IS 10500 for the first time in the system.
- Nitrate and fluoride were identified as the real health issues, with 32 wells and 14 districts affected.
- “Not tested” was made a separate grey class, so that missing data is never shown as safe.
- The public map shows each district by its worst measured health substance.

In the 2000–2021 chemistry record, electrical conductivity is rising at 13 wells and falling at 6.

## 7.3 Machine Learning Model Performance

The final surrogate (version 5) was tested with 5-fold cross-validation grouped by scenario, on 18,000 rows from 900 scenarios.

TABLE: Surrogate model performance (P50 model, cross-validation)
|{w=4.2,2.4,2.8,3.0,3.6}|
|Target|R² (log scale)|Scenario coverage (target ≥ 0.80)|Ridge baseline R² (log)|Average error (MAE)|
|Footprint area|**0.893**|0.863|0.561|6.91 ha|
|Travel distance|**0.926**|0.878|0.773|68.8 m|
|Ring concentration|**0.947**|0.866|0.744|mixed units|
|Excursion probability|0.915 (R²)|–|–|0.049|

The surrogate is better than all the baseline models on every target. When whole aquifer polygons were held out (leave-aquifer-out), the R² values were 0.890, 0.921 and 0.941, only slightly lower, which shows that the model works on geology it has not seen. On a separate test batch of 120 scenarios placed on real Jharkhand flow and fracture values, the 80% bands covered 0.885, 0.875 and 0.881 of scenarios for the three targets, so all three passed.

TABLE: R² (log scale) of the P50 model for each chemical (target ≥ 0.60)
|{w=4.4,2.9,2.9,2.9,2.9}|
|Target|Uranium|Sulphate|TDS|Radium-226|
|Footprint area|0.943|0.789|0.820|0.892|
|Travel distance|0.930|0.878|0.884|**0.500 (below target)**|
|Ring concentration|0.847|0.917|0.962|**0.235 (below target)**|

For radium, two results are below the project’s own target of 0.60. This is because radium hardly moves: 81.8% of its travel-distance values are exactly zero and 95.8% of its ring values equal the background. A regression model cannot learn such data well. The result is reported openly instead of lowering the target. The radium uncertainty bands still cover more than 92% of cases, and the physics engine always gives the main radium value, so users are not misled.

SHAP analysis showed that the model relies on the right physical quantities. The plume front position calculated by the engine is the strongest feature for travel distance and excursion probability. The source concentration and wellfield width are the strongest for the footprint area, because most of the footprint is the leach zone itself. No map coordinate can appear, because none is a feature.

![SHAP feature importance for the excursion-probability model (version 5): the plume front position calculated by the engine (Xc_m) dominates.](docs/report/figures/fig_shap_pex.png){w=13}

### Key achievements include:

- Surrogate R² (log) of 0.89 to 0.95, better than all baselines.
- 80% coverage achieved on real-location test scenarios for all three targets.
- Physical rules built into the model and checked against the engine.
- Radium weakness reported openly, with the engine used as the authority.

## 7.4 Validation of the Transport Engine

In the second review round, a reviewer suggested that the Domenico approximation could cause errors of up to 80% [@west2007]. To test this, the engine was compared with an exact numerical solution on 240 parameter sets drawn from the training data. The result disproved the concern but found a different problem.

TABLE: Error of the engine along the plume centreline compared with the exact solution
|{w=5.5,5.2,5.3}|
|Position along the plume|Domenico approximation only|Version then in use (missing term)|
|Half-way to the front|0.00%|−16.9% (typical)|
|At the front|0.00%|−22.4% (typical)|
|Beyond the front|−0.10% to 0.00%|−24.3% (typical)|

The Domenico approximation itself caused no error in this project’s range of parameters. However, the version then in use had dropped the second term of the Ogata–Banks formula [@ogata1961], which made every concentration 17% to 42% too low. This was the opposite of the safe (conservative) side the tool aims for. The full term was restored and the surrogate was retrained on the exact formula. The end-to-end audit of the final version passes 43 of 44 checks; the only failure is the radium target described above.

## 7.5 Case Study: Hypothetical ISR Site at Jaduguda

The engine was run for a hypothetical ISR operation at the Jaduguda deposit, with 8 years of operation, an injection rate of 2,500 m³/day, 2% bleed, a 300 m wide wellfield, no restoration and a monitoring ring 100 m from the wellfield. The location resolves to fractured rock in the shear zone, with a gradient of 0.00205, K of 0.563 m/day at ore depth and β of 3.0.

TABLE: Modelled results at the Jaduguda deposit after 20 years (engine value with ML band)
|{w=2.6,2.8,2.6,2.6,2.2,3.2}|
|Chemical|Source|Footprint (ha)|Travel distance (m)|Excursion probability|ML band for distance, P10–P90 (m)|
|Uranium|15,180 ppb|9.60|**20.5**|0.00|2.7 – 100|
|Sulphate|1,624.5 mg/L|11.25|**73.1**|0.27|3.5 – 467|
|TDS|3,655.5 mg/L|17.62|**254**|0.96|10.5 – 3,043|
|Radium-226|1,706 mBq/L|9.06|0.6|0.00|0.0 – 2.3|

The order of spread (uranium far behind sulphate, which is behind TDS) comes from how strongly each chemical is held back by the rock (R_eff ≈ 271 for uranium, 17.3 for sulphate and 4.0 for TDS). This is the same reason the US rules do not use uranium as an excursion indicator [@nureg1569], and the engine reproduces it without being told. At a location with no uranium ore (Ranchi), no uranium plume is drawn, while sulphate and TDS still spread, because the lixiviant carries them wherever it is injected.

For the hypothetical site registered on the deployed system (just outside the Jaduguda deposit, injection 1,500 m³/day), a lifecycle run was made over 50 years:

TABLE: Lifecycle of the registered Jaduguda site (no restoration)
|{w=1.6,2.8,2.6,3.2,2.6,3.2}|
|Year|Phase|Footprint (ha)|Uranium travel (m)|Shallow aquifer index|Excursion declared|
|4|operation|8.13|11.1|0.16|no|
|8|closure|8.65|12.5|0.28|no|
|12|after closure|8.82|16.6|0.39|yes (Cl, TDS)|
|20|after closure|8.99|22.7|0.62|yes (Cl, TDS)|
|30|after closure|9.16|28.5|0.91|yes (Cl, TDS, SO₄)|
|50|after closure|9.51|37.8|1.00|yes (Cl, TDS, SO₄)|

Uranium never reaches the 100 m ring in 50 years, but an excursion is declared from year 12, because chloride and TDS arrive first. The chemical indicators give the early warning, exactly as intended. When a 6-year restoration sweep is added, the 20-year uranium travel falls to 4.2 m and no excursion is declared (only chloride stays above its control limit).

**Shallow aquifer screening.** At this site, the ore top is at 140 m and the base of the shallow layer at 20 m. The leakage pathway dominates, and the combined index is **0.62 (high)**. The estimated breakthrough time is **18.7 years** on the yearly-average basis, 6.8 years in the dry-season case, and not expected in the wet-season case. This is a screening of whether a pathway exists, not a prediction of concentration in drinking water.

## 7.7 Sensitivity Analysis Results

The Sobol sensitivity analysis showed which inputs control the results. At the Jaduguda deposit, the travel distance of uranium depends mostly on hydraulic conductivity K (index 0.42), then β (0.21), then the gradient (0.16). At the belt location, β becomes the most important (0.29). The footprint area depends mostly on the two leach-zone growth constants, which are project assumptions. The fracture opening and the matrix diffusion coefficient have almost no effect at 20 years.

This is an important result for future work. The two inputs that decide the plume extent, K and β, are not measured at ore depth anywhere in the Singhbhum shear zone. K comes from the shallow aquifer map with a depth law, and β comes from typical porosities. A field tracer test in the shear zone would reduce this uncertainty more than anything else.

## 7.8 Alert System Results

On the local database, the measured-exceedance scan raised **53 alerts** from the 2023 record:

- **3 critical:** Biru (Simdega, nitrate 121 mg/L, 2.7 times the limit), Lowadih (Ranchi, nitrate 99 mg/L) and Gidhaur (Chatra, above both the nitrate and fluoride limits)
- **29 alert:** other wells above a permissible or “no relaxation” limit
- **21 warning:** fluoride between 1.0 and 1.5 mg/L

Before these fixes, the same scan had matched zero rows every time, because it only checked uranium above 30 ppb, which never happens in this record. On the deployed system (21 September 2026), 55 alerts had been raised, 4 residents were following 7 blocks, and email delivery through the Brevo relay was working.
