# 6. WORK CARRIED OUT DURING FELLOWSHIP

The fellowship ran from November 2025 to August 2026, with a finishing phase in September 2026. Each month is summarised from the monthly progress reports (MPRs) and the code history.

## 6.1 November 2025: Literature Study and Project Setup

The month was spent on literature [@mda2011; @saunders2016] and on setting up the platform.

### Activities Performed

- Study of monitoring network design, aquifer vulnerability frameworks and ISR contamination pathways
- Design of the overall system architecture (frontend, backend API, ML service, database and cache)
- Setting up the development environment (Node.js, PostgreSQL, Redis, Python, Docker)
- Development of the first admin panel with JWT login and three roles

## 6.2 December 2025: Move to FastAPI and Spatial Database

The backend was moved to FastAPI, and a simulation workflow was set up with a placeholder output to be replaced by a real model later.

### Activities Performed

- Migration of the backend from Node.js to FastAPI
- Design of the PostgreSQL + PostGIS database schema (13 tables) with Alembic migrations
- REST APIs for users, districts, blocks and aquifers, with Pydantic input checks
- Role-based access control and JWT authentication

## 6.3 January 2026: First Real CGWB Data

The aquifer model was extended with CGWB fields such as transmissivity, fractures encountered and yield range.

### Activities Performed

- Mapping of CGWB dataset columns to the database
- Loading of Dhanbad groundwater-level data
- ISR points API with coordinates stored in PostGIS
- Data ingestion API for district GeoJSON and aquifer Excel files
- Frontend connected to the backend, with a Leaflet map of districts and aquifers

## 6.4 February 2026: Complete Data Layer

All five Jharkhand datasets were loaded in one step: 25 districts, 275 blocks, 24 aquifer polygons, 397 monitoring wells and 397 water samples. The data-quality report gave the first data-gap finding: iron and arsenic were missing from every sample.

### Activities Performed

- Database migration for wells, samples and data provenance
- Five ingestion pipelines that parse range values such as “26 - 176” and “2-3%”, fill missing values from the literature [@freeze1979] and use SHA-256 checksums against duplicate loads
- One-step seeding script with a data-quality report

![The admin dashboard of the early platform (February 2026).](assets/feb_admin_dashboard.png){w=12}

## 6.5 March 2026: First Machine Learning Pipeline

The real data had very few unsafe samples, so synthetic ones were generated for the first models.

### Activities Performed

- 16-feature schema (aquifer properties, distance to the nearest ISR point, time, a simple vulnerability value) extracted with one PostGIS query
- Gaussian-copula synthetic data generator (NumPy and SciPy)
- TDS regressor (Random Forest and Ridge) and contamination classifier (Random Forest and Logistic Regression) with 5-fold cross-validation
- Prediction service returning a risk score for each simulation

![SHAP feature importance of the March 2026 contamination classifier. This pipeline was later replaced (Section 6.8).](assets/mar_shap_classifier.png){w=12}

## 6.6 April 2026: International ISR Data

Since ISR is not carried out in India, the mentor advised looking for internationally published ISR data. The USGS Texas ISR dataset [@usgs_tx] was chosen because it covers three stages of mining: before mining, at the end of mining and after restoration.

### Activities Performed

- Survey of USGS, EPA and CGWB datasets for ISR groundwater data
- Cleaning of the Texas file, including values such as “<.001” and “1044±5” (186 usable rows)
- Synthetic dataset of 5,186 rows with contamination falling with distance and seasonal effects
- Random Forest risk classifier (91.1% test accuracy, 0.90 macro-F1)

![Training output of the April 2026 Random Forest risk model.](assets/apr_rf_output.png){w=11}

## 6.7 May 2026: Uranium as the Prediction Target

Uranium values had been dropped in the earlier work, so a single pipeline was built with uranium as the real target. Its uranium model reported an R² of 0.69 (log scale) on real rows.

### Activities Performed

- Unified pipeline on 342 real Jharkhand rows, 131 Texas rows and 3,000 synthetic rows, each tagged by origin
- Five models: uranium, TDS, sulphate and pH regressors and a safe/marginal/unsafe classifier
- Simplification of the backend (Celery and Redis removed) and a two-command database setup

## 6.8 June 2026: Audit and Rebuild with a Physics Engine

June was the turning point. An audit found that the May model’s R² of 0.69 measured natural background uranium, not contamination spread (Section 9.3), and the pipeline was replaced by a physics engine with an ML surrogate trained on its output (Sections 4.5 and 4.7).

### Activities Performed

- Audit of the earlier ML approach and decision to rebuild
- 2-D transport engine: Domenico solution [@domenico1987], scale-dependent dispersion [@xu1995], matrix diffusion [@tang1981], dual-porosity slowing [@goltz1986], bleed containment and uranium re-trapping [@reimus2019] over the full mine life
- 13,500 physics-labelled training rows from 900 scenarios on real aquifer polygons; ten XGBoost models with monotone constraints and conformal bands
- Grounding in real Jharkhand flow, fracture, layer, deposit and river data
- Interactive dashboard, and a sweep of about 1,100 cases at nine locations that fixed four defects

![The June 2026 engine dashboard: a hypothetical ISR site on the Jharkhand map with its modelled plume.](assets/jun_engine_dashboard.png){w=12}

## 6.9 July 2026: Review Rounds and Backend Rebuild

The engine went through three review rounds (Section 9.5), the surrogate’s travel-distance R² rose from 0.72 to 0.90 over four retrains, and the backend was rebuilt on the real engine.

### Activities Performed

- Depth-dependent permeability from NAQUIM, mineralogy-based uranium attenuation and boundary smoothing
- Radium-226 added (18,000 training rows) and a chloride indicator for the excursion test
- Signup role hole closed; five roles enforced by the API and row-level security, with an audit log
- Field-observation workflow; boundaries stored with latitude and longitude swapped since February fixed
- New role-based React and TypeScript portal

## 6.10 August 2026: Complete Product and Deployment

The portal was completed and the system was deployed. The 397-well record was assessed against IS 10500:2012 (Section 7.1), and three security controls that had been configured but were not working were found and fixed (Section 9.9). The final presentation was given to the TEXMiN–BIT Sindri review panel.

### Activities Performed

- Completion of the 22-screen portal: console, publication, My Area, alerts, site report with PDF export, site comparison, saved scenarios, dataset manager, network plan and public front page
- IS 10500 assessment of the measured record
- Water-level trends, monitoring-gap ranking, and daily email alerts to residents’ home blocks
- Deployment on Neon, Render and Cloudflare Workers, with a restricted database role
- Final presentation to the review panel

## 6.11 September 2026: Final Improvements and Report

After the fellowship period, the model inputs for the uranium belt were checked against data from the belt itself. The most important finding was that the shear-zone transmissivity used until then, 370 m²/day, came from wells outside the belt; it was replaced by the measured 19 m²/day at Kudada, and every run was given two answers (Sections 4.4, 4.5 and 9.6). An external review of the report then led to the preferential-pathway answer and the measured upward gradient (Section 7.5b).

### Activities Performed

- β from local porosities; alert tiers with seven-field explanations; timelines, hydrochemical QA and the 2000–2021 chemistry record; email retry fixed
- Vertical pathway rebuilt to carry the dissolved chemical, with a measured Kv/Kh (Section 4.6)
- Continuous plume raster and chance-over-limit map, fanned by the measured direction uncertainty
- CGWB 2024 water quality, 1994–2026 water levels and belt boreholes extracted; 3-D site block
- Shear-zone permeability, uranium and radium backgrounds, radium Kd and ore depths from local sources
- Surrogate version 6 (half the scenarios on the tight rock of the deposits; radium ring-concentration R² 0.235 → 0.746), sensitivity analysis, and on 27 September the preferential-pathway answer, high-alkalinity scenario, measured upward gradient and injection check
